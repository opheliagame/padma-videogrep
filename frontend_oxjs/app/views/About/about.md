# 1

the human thought process is not linear. it jumps, turns, hops, moves around instead of travelling along a straight line. while stories as we know them most popularly, are most structured. a beginning, a middle, an end. three acts, en exposition, a character arc, a climax.

From historical accounts to business presentations to politicians; almost everyone knows how important a good structure is, if you want to have the audience believe your version of reality. When storytelling serves a purpose, this is what it looks like. An edit of raw reality, placed in an order that makes linear sense and travels respectfully from introduction to conclusion. The editing table can both be exciting and limiting. Raw reality, though, is everything but limiting.

# 2

**padma videogrep** is a (one) way (tool) to look at this raw reality. a specific raw reality that exists inside a database. similar to how most of your life exists inside computational systems. an organisation of personal information such that it can be stored, accessed, managed, updated and searched electronically.

# 3

**padma videogrep** is a (one) way of writing / editing / watching stories via the connections that exist within records in the database, the hops between nodes in a network. it is a found media collage, it is database cinema [^1] as Lev Manovich imagined it.

# 4

the urge to categorise and label is a very colonial instinct. keeping archives of human experiences and making artifacts of them is one way in which we present our lives to the machine. and the machine in turn is immensely capable, as of recent years, to apply meticulous transformations on this information and extract intelligence from it. what is in this image that i took this morning during breakfast? is it an apple or an orange? or it is [ragi mudde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragi_mudde)? someone has taught the ghost in my machine what an apple looks like. and possibly by taking a picture of my breakfast [i am also teaching the mother ghost](https://support.google.com/crowdsource/answer/13827497?hl=en) about ragi mudde.

the epidemiology of this giving away our lives to the machines are many and people far more articulate with their words have said many a words about it. so i will turn our focus on what might happen once the black box has performed its' magic and left us with crumbs of intelligence.

the human urge to connect with other people plays out on our mobile devices a lot. be it finding someone to spend time romantically with, or speak with friends from school, we all grab that one thing which almost always is by our side. there are feeds where [the mother ghost knows us better than our mom](https://youtu.be/gMC3gEcD3R0?si=fDJF4R4-7-dLD_-G&t=53), there is chatter, there are connections and mutual friends and recommendations and people you may know. there is an orchestration of cheese supported on top of a crumbly intelligence crust. and even though we may think we are, we are not the ones in control. it is a part of the performance to make us believe that we are.

the act to catalogue has its' beginnings in a colonial act. and as an annotator and archivist, one may use machines, the ultimate colonial tool, to gather **machine intelligence** and inject it into the archive. that is one way to make meaning; one way to know and look at the world we are swimming in.

**padma videogrep** is a (one) way to cut through and stick together the many slices of visual memory and sounds living in the database. to turn around the contents and look at their many angles, one at a time. to swim in the archive.

## what is pad.ma

Pad.ma - short for Public Access Digital Media Archive - is an online archive of densely text-annotated video material, primarily footage and not finished films. The entire collection is searchable and viewable online and is free to download for non-commercial use.

https://pad.ma/about

## what is videogrep

Videogrep is a command line tool that searches through dialog in video or audio files and makes supercuts based on what it finds. It will recognize .srt or .vtt subtitle tracks, or transcriptions that can be generated with vosk, pocketsphinx, and other tools.

http://antiboredom.github.io/videogrep/

# an appendix

I have always been fascinated by systems. Maybe it is because I have had a hard time saying the things that I want to say; so instead I turn to writing and defining rules, formulating and conspiring a game, a tool, a plaything, something that you could speak into and it talks back. This thing can say the things I might have wanted to say. This thing has decision making. It has opinions. It has agency.

I have relocated my agency into this system. So it can go and have a life of its' own. It can change if it wants. It can adapt to the people it meets. It can be a part of me outside of me.

> ⎊

> going back to being a kid
> having a kid to be able to get one's voice back
> making a virtual kid

Storytellers can have many reasons for telling stories but one of them is to make you believe in their version of reality, how they see the world. I have never had this urge; or at least it has not been strong enough.

I am not what you might call a storyteller. my words are more like tangents, each turning abruptly onto the next street, sometimes racing past windows, only to linger at intersections.

I am what you might call a forever observer, a voyeur, a lingerer; a bathroom singer not bound by what the guitarist or the drummer wants to do but instead trusting only in the water and the steam and if i am washing my hair or not.

## How to use

### Getting started

1. Type a query into the scene box.
2. Press **Enter** to add the next scene.
3. Click the title in the header to rename your screenplay.
4. Press **Play** to watch clips, scene by scene.
5. Press **Write** to go back and edit.

### Writing queries

| Query                       | Finds clips that mention…         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `water`                     | water                             |
| `climate change`            | the phrase "climate change"       |
| `water and rain`            | both water and rain               |
| `water or rain`             | either water or rain              |
| `(water and river) or rain` | water and river together, or rain |

- Words next to each other form one phrase — no quotes needed.
- `and` / `AND` and `or` / `OR` both work. `and` is applied before `or`; use brackets to group.
- Use letters, numbers and `_` only. Quotes and punctuation are not supported.

### Generativity controls

- **length** — clip duration: short (under 5s), medium (5–15s) or long (over 15s).
- **sort** — order of clips: relevance, date or random.
- **clip count** — how many clips per scene, from 1 to 50 (default 10).

### Where clips come from

Clips come from the [pad.ma](https://pad.ma) archive. A query matches text in a video's transcripts, keywords, places, events and descriptions.
